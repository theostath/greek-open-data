What this can answer

{# A sequence the reader genuinely needs: it is what the progress screen will narrate while they wait, and the three ways a question can end. #}

  1. It finds a dataset

    Your wording is matched against every catalogue entry, then checked: does this dataset really cover the question?

  2. It reads the data

    The figure is computed from the publisher's own CSV or JSON file, never estimated or written by the model.

  3. It cites, or it declines

    Every answer names the dataset, publisher and date. Roughly half of questions find nothing — and you are told so, with the nearest datasets.

What it will not do
  • Invent a figure. If no dataset covers your question, it says so and shows you what it looked at.
  • Join two datasets together. Greek regional codes changed with the Kapodistrias–Kallikratis reforms, so joining on region names would produce confident nonsense.
  • Hide staleness. Some datasets have not been updated in years; the answer says which and when.
  • Read PDFs or spreadsheets. Only CSV and JSON resources can be quoted from here.
